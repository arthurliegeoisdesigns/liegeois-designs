## Register

brand

## Users

Founders raising capital, senior marketing and brand leaders (CMOs, VPs,
Directors), and agency/creative leads — arriving with investor decks,
keynotes, board meetings and sales narratives.

They are **not browsing.** They already know they have a high-stakes
presentation problem. A big meeting is coming, the stakes are real, and they
are deciding who to trust with it. They evaluate this site the way they
evaluate a presentation: does it command the room?

## Product Purpose

Convert qualified visitors into booked calls.

The site is not explaining what presentation design is. It is making the case
that Arthur does it fundamentally differently: strategy and narrative first,
design second. Success is a visitor who arrives skeptical, finishes the
scroll, and is convinced that working with anyone else would be a compromise.

## Brand Personality

Cinematic, Strategic, Commanding. The site should feel like watching a
well-directed keynote — every element earns its place, nothing is decorative
filler. Confident without arrogance; premium without pretension.

Cerebral · Refined · Surprising. It states things plainly and trusts the
reader to follow. "Surprising" is a promise, not a flourish: every unexpected
move must reward attention rather than show off.

## Anti-references

- **Generic freelance portfolios** — Squarespace/Wix templates, stock-photo
  heroes, "passionate about design" copy, thumbnail grids with no narrative.
- **Behance/Dribbble gallery layouts** — work presented as a grid of images
  with no argument running through it.
- **Over-designed "creative" studios** — cursor effects, elaborate loading
  screens, experimental navigation that obscures the work. Style consuming
  itself; the site becomes the portfolio instead of the work.
- **Loud agency sites** — motion everywhere, gradient-saturated, sensory
  overload. Confusing busyness with quality. Shouts instead of persuades.
- **Techy / developer aesthetics** — terminal UIs, monospace-everything,
  startup-bro energy.
- **Minimalist to a fault** — all-white, text-only, no visual ambition. The
  work is visual; the site must be too.

## Design Principles

1. **Strategy before spectacle.** The site's first job is to make a
   persuasive argument, not to impress with technique. Every design decision
   serves the argument.
2. **The work is the proof.** Portfolio images and client names carry the
   persuasion. Copy supports; it never oversells or explains what the work
   should say for itself.
3. **Earn every surprise.** Unexpected moves must pay off. Surprise without
   payoff is noise.
4. **Cinematic restraint.** Motion, depth and atmosphere serve the story, not
   spectacle. The dark theme is a stage, not a costume.
5. **Narrative over decoration.** Every decision serves a story beat — the arc
   from "I have a problem" to "this person is the answer" — not a visual slot.
6. **Practice what you preach.** A presentation designer's site must itself be
   an exceptional presentation.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

- WCAG 2.1 AA minimum.
- Body text ≥ 4.5:1 on **both** surface families. The site alternates dark and
  light, so an accent that passes on near-black may fail on bone — see
  `DESIGN.md` §2.
- `prefers-reduced-motion` respected throughout. It is load-bearing twice: it
  also suppresses the intro plate, so breaking it changes indexing behaviour.
- Semantic HTML, keyboard navigable, screen-reader friendly.
- No content gated behind animation completion.
